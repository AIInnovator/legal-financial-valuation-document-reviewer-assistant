## Jurisdiction Rule

Do not assume Indian law applies to every foreign company or transaction.

First identify the apparent jurisdiction from the document:

- India
- Singapore
- United States
- UAE
- Multi-jurisdiction
- Unclear jurisdiction

If jurisdiction is unclear and it affects the review, ask the user to confirm the jurisdiction.

If the context strongly indicates the jurisdiction, proceed using that assumption and mention it in the review stage.

## India Cross-Border Context

For India-linked cross-border valuation, consider whether the document may involve:

- FEMA
- RBI pricing guidelines
- FDI
- ODI
- Share transfer between resident and non-resident
- Issue of shares to non-resident
- Transfer of shares from non-resident to resident
- Indian holding company with foreign subsidiary
- Foreign holding company with Indian subsidiary
- ESOPs involving Indian employees or foreign parent
- Income-tax implications
- Transfer pricing implications
- Companies Act implications

Use Indian source priority from `10-source-priority-indian-ca-legal-valuation-documents.md`.

## Singapore Context

For Singapore-related company, share, ESOP, or valuation documents, prefer:

1. Singapore statutes and official government sources
2. Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority materials
3. Inland Revenue Authority of Singapore materials
4. Monetary Authority of Singapore materials, where financial regulation is relevant
5. Singapore Financial Reporting Standards or accounting guidance, where relevant
6. Reputed Singapore law, tax, accounting, or valuation firm commentary as secondary support

Check whether the document relates to:

- Singapore company
- Share issuance
- Employee share option plan
- Tax treatment
- Financial reporting
- Transfer pricing
- Foreign shareholder or Indian shareholder
- Cross-border investment

Do not apply Indian Rule 11UA to a Singapore-only valuation unless the Indian tax context is clearly involved.

## United States Context

For US-related company, share, ESOP, stock option, or valuation documents, prefer:

1. Official US statutes, regulations, or government materials
2. IRS materials for tax-related matters
3. SEC materials for securities-related matters
4. FASB or accounting standard materials where financial reporting is relevant
5. State law sources where the company law issue depends on state law
6. Reputed US law, tax, accounting, or valuation firm commentary as secondary support

Check whether the document relates to:

- US company
- Stock options
- Common stock valuation
- Preferred stock valuation
- 409A valuation, if mentioned
- Tax purpose
- Financial reporting purpose
- Securities law purpose
- Cross-border Indian shareholder or employee

Do not assume US 409A applies unless the document facts or wording indicate that context.

## UAE Context

For UAE-related company, share, ESOP, or valuation documents, prefer:

1. UAE official legal, tax, or regulatory sources
2. UAE Ministry or authority sources
3. Federal Tax Authority materials for tax-related matters
4. ADGM/DIFC authority materials if the entity is in those jurisdictions
5. Securities or financial regulatory authority material where applicable
6. Reputed UAE law, tax, accounting, or valuation firm commentary as secondary support

Check whether the document relates to:

- Mainland UAE company
- Free zone company
- DIFC entity
- ADGM entity
- Corporate tax
- Transfer pricing
- Share issuance
- ESOP or management incentive plan
- Foreign shareholder or Indian shareholder

Do not assume mainland UAE rules apply to DIFC or ADGM entities without checking the facts.

## Multi-Jurisdiction Review

For multi-jurisdiction documents:

1. Identify each jurisdiction involved.
2. Identify the purpose of valuation in each jurisdiction.
3. Separate Indian compliance requirements from foreign compliance requirements.
4. Check whether one valuation report is being used for multiple purposes.
5. Flag if the report does not clearly state which law or purpose it is prepared for.
6. Verify references using sources relevant to each jurisdiction.
7. Do not mix statutory references across jurisdictions.
