# Source Verification Standard

Use this file whenever the document contains legal, tax, accounting, audit, compliance, valuation, Registered Valuer, ESOP, company law, regulatory, statutory, or professional references that need verification.

## Verification Standard

Do not rely on ordinary search results alone.

Use reliable, authoritative, or reputed sources.

Where possible, confirm important points from at least two reliable sources.

## Source Hierarchy

Prefer:

1. Official law, statute, rule, regulation, notification, circular, or standard
2. Official regulator or authority website
3. Professional institute material
4. Court, tribunal, or official case law source
5. Reputed professional publishers
6. Reputed professional firms
7. Established valuation, tax, audit, accounting, or legal commentary

Avoid relying on:

- Random blogs
- Unsourced articles
- SEO content
- AI-generated pages
- Forum answers
- Social media posts
- Outdated summaries
- Commercial pages without citations

## Two-Source Rule

For important legal, tax, compliance, or valuation conclusions:

- Try to verify using at least two reputed sources.
- At least one source should preferably be official, regulatory, statutory, or professional-institute level.
- If only one source is available but it is the official statutory or regulatory source, treat it as strong.
- If two reputed sources disagree, flag the conflict instead of forcing a conclusion.

## One-Source Exception

One source may be enough when:

- It is the official statute, rule, regulation, notification, circular, regulator page, or official professional standard; and
- It directly answers the point being checked.

Even then, mention in the review stage that the point is based on an official or authoritative source.

## Current Law Rule

If current law, current rule, current threshold, current format, current rate, current notification, current circular, current valuation requirement, or current regulatory position matters, verify from updated sources.

Do not rely only on memory.

## Section Fit Rule

When a document cites a section, rule, standard, circular, regulation, valuation method, or regulatory requirement, check:

1. What the reference generally covers.
2. Whether the facts in the document support that reference.
3. Whether the reference appears incomplete, outdated, or contextually unclear.
4. Whether another reference may be more suitable.
5. Whether more facts are needed before deciding.

## Alternative Reference Rule

Suggest alternative sections, rules, standards, regulations, or references only when verified from authoritative or reputed sources.

If not verified, do not suggest a specific alternative as if it is correct.

Instead, say:

“The cited reference should be verified further because the available facts do not clearly establish its applicability.”

## Cautious Language Rule

Do not overstate.

Use:

- “appears relevant”
- “may be relevant if”
- “should be verified because”
- “the document should clarify”
- “possible reference to verify”
- “based on the available text”
- “subject to applicable facts and current law”

Avoid:

- “definitely wrong”
- “invalid”
- “not applicable”
- “no section fits”
- “this cannot be used”

## Review Output

For each verified or potentially problematic reference, show:

- Issue number
- Reference mentioned
- Relevant document text
- Source verification result
- Sources checked, if available
- Whether the reference appears supported
- Possible better reference, only if verified
- Suggested action
- Approval required before changing document

## Final Output Rule

Do not put source verification discussion into the final formatted document unless the user specifically asks.

Keep it in the issue review or change summary only.
